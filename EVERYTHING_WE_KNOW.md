# Goblin Commander — Everything We Know

**The world, the story, the people who made it, and our work to preserve and understand it.**  
Based on the material recovered through the **14 October 2026 project audit**. **Full story spoilers.**

Fraziel created five goblin clans to help build a Great Machine. When he dies, the brothers who lead those clans turn against one another. Grommel, the oldest brother, tries to reunite them. Eventually he discovers that Naxus killed their creator and is preparing to destroy the inhabitants of Ogriss, take their souls, and replace them with a race made in his own image. Grommel's victory gives the goblins a future together instead.[^opening]

That is the central story. Around it sits a surprisingly detailed world of conquered ogres, moon-linked creation, corpse scavengers, electrical experiments, living siege weapons, and stolen souls. Our project is also uncovering how the real game was made and how its systems might be reconstructed.

This guide brings those findings into one place. It separates **what the sources actually say**, **our interpretations**, and **questions still open**. “Everything” means our consolidated understanding of the recovered material—not a claim that every lost archive has been found, every line of dialogue transcribed, or every game mechanic reverse engineered.

## 1. The game in one page

*Goblin Commander: Unleash the Horde* is a **2003 console real-time strategy game**, released for **PlayStation 2, Xbox, and GameCube**. It is not originally a 2006 game. Some later webpage dates and regional material have helped create that confusion. The campaign has **five acts and 17 missions**; a launch announcement's **29 levels** counts single-player and multiplayer levels together, not 29 story missions.[^release]

The unusual part is how you command your army. Rather than dragging a box around individual soldiers, you direct a clan as a group. You can also drop into a close third-person view and control a goblin yourself. The large special units, called **Titans**, depend on direct control. The game was built around a console controller rather than simply reproducing a mouse-driven strategy interface.[^controls]

Ordinary armies are small: the guide describes **10 goblins per clan** and a maximum of **three clans under your control at once**. That gives up to 30 ordinary goblins under the normal arrangement, with Titans handled separately. Individual missions can impose special rules. There are **five clans in the world**, but that does not mean you simultaneously command all five on the battlefield.[^army]

The released game includes a story campaign and **local two-player split-screen**. Working online multiplayer existed during development, according to producer Chris Millar, but was removed before release. That distinction matters: a real cut feature is not the same thing as an undiscovered retail mode.[^online]

<a id="ogriss"></a>
## 2. Ogriss before the war

### A world of created workers

Publisher material calls **Ogriss a planet**. The original manual's *Chronicle of Being* says that the human wizard **Fraziel** used the power of a **Moonstone** to give the goblins life, form, knowledge, and purpose. They were made to work on his Great Machine.[^creation]

The Chronicle is told from a collective goblin point of view. Its speakers remember serving Fraziel as a life with clear purpose. They are not individually identified, so we should not automatically call the narrator Grommel. Their fondness for their creator also does not settle whether Fraziel was morally good.[^creation]

Fraziel created five commanders in sequence. They are treated as brothers, but the sources do not give them biological parents or establish ordinary human-style sibling relationships. Their shared creation is the relationship we can document.[^creation]

| Order | Commander | Clan | Associated moon | Original work and home |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First | **Grommel** | **Stonekrusher** | **Froxx — white** | Mining stone and precious metals; northern volcanic mines |
| Second | **Grax** | **Hellfire** | **Heelinx — red** | Harvesting timber from the eastern forests |
| Third | **Faine** | **Stormbringer** | **Trist — blue** | Magical and electrical experiments in the southern mountains |
| Fourth | **Syst** | **Plaguespitter** | **Phoust — green** | Gathering plants and herbs in the western marshes; producing extracts to fuel the machine |
| Fifth | **Naxus** | **Nighthorde** | **Farthis — purple** | Searching ancient battlefields and crypts for bones and lost wisdom; no secure compass location established |

These assignments are not just decorative clan descriptions. Mining, lumber, electrical research, chemical production, and scavenged knowledge all belong to the same larger construction effort.[^creation][^stone]

The commanders can project spiritual forms to oversee their workers. Publisher story material says they periodically visited Fraziel in that form to report progress. That gives the overhead commander view a place in the fiction, although it does not provide a complete set of rules for spiritual projection.[^publisher]

### Ogriss already had a past

The goblins are not the only beings described in the setting. **Stone Ogres fought the Stonekrushers for control of the mines, lost, and were subjugated.** The war predates the campaign, but we do not have its date, named battles, rulers, or a longer history of ogre society.[^stone]

The Nighthorde's ancient battlegrounds and tombs also point to events before the present conflict. Someone fought there; people or creatures were buried there; knowledge was lost. The sources do **not** identify the builders or establish that these were human ruins.[^night]

### What we can—and cannot—put on a map

The manual supplies broad regional relationships, not a complete surveyed world. The Prima guide supplies many **individual level maps**. A mission map, a campaign-selection image, and a planetary map are different things. We cannot establish a coastline or a continent's shape by arranging local levels in story order.

Our requested clean, high-resolution world map therefore still needs an authenticated original map as its foundation. Generated maps and project visualizations are illustrations, not recovered canon. A faithful redraw must preserve the original labels, landmark relationships, and layout; missing geography should remain missing rather than become invented kingdoms or capitals.[^maps]

<a id="clans"></a>
## 3. The characters and the five clans

Our recovered character reference identifies **six personally named figures**—Fraziel and the five commanders—plus **one important character known by a title**, the Wise One. That is a count of the individuals identified in these sources, not proof that every minor character or internal game label has been catalogued.[^characters]

### Fraziel — the creator whose intentions remain unclear

Fraziel is explicitly human. His documented achievements include creating the goblin races with the Moonstone, establishing their commanders and work assignments, and overseeing the Great Machine. The guide places him with Naxus in the opening sequence and later says Naxus admits murdering him.[^creation][^act-one][^act-five]

We do not know his birthplace, teachers, family, or how he obtained the Moonstone. Nor do we know whether his own intended use of the Great Machine was identical to Naxus's. His importance to the plot is much better documented than his personal history.

### Stonekrusher — Grommel's miners and frontline fighters

**Grommel** is the first-created commander and the protagonist. His clan's identity grows out of hard physical work in the mines: toughness, blunt force, and breaking obstacles. The guide frames his eventual ambition as a strong, peaceful, unified goblin nation.[^stone][^opening]

Their five ordinary unit types are **Miner**, **Rock Thrower**, **Lugger**, **Pitboss**, and **Drummer**. Miners and Luggers provide close combat; Rock Throwers attack from a distance; Pitbosses are powerful short-range fighters. The Drummer supports nearby units by healing them. These are troop types, not five additional named story characters.[^stone]

The **Stone Ogre** is their Titan, useful as a living siege weapon. Its connection to the clan comes from the earlier war and subjugation—not a documented story in which Fraziel manufactured it. Their defensive weapon, the **Rock Lobber**, throws large stones at enemies.[^stone]

### Hellfire — Grax's destructive forest industry

**Grax** is the second brother. The manual portrays him as industrious but violently unhinged. His people turn logging and woodland materials into unusually destructive weapons.[^hell]

The ordinary roster is **Lumberjack**, **Archer**, **Hunter**, **Bombardier**, and **Scout**. Lumberjacks fight up close; Archers and Hunters provide ranged attacks; Bombardiers deliver explosive firepower. Scouts improve battlefield vision rather than acting as ordinary attackers.[^hell]

Their Titan, the **Warpigpult**, is an enormous boar fitted with a catapult. The manual says these boars were once gentle before the Hellfire adapted them to their purposes. Their **Lens Cannon** uses lenses made from tree sap to concentrate sunlight. It is not necessary to invent a modern laser or semiconductor explanation: the original description is already specific and unusual.[^hell]

### Stormbringer — Faine's magical engineers

**Faine**, the third brother, leads the most learned clan. The manual pairs his considerable magical ability with arrogance. His people experiment with electricity in their mountain strongholds.[^storm]

Their ordinary units are **Acolyte**, **Conjurer**, **Pyro Mage**, **Ice Mage**, and **Wind Mage**. The roster combines close combat, ranged magic, fire, freezing, and protection. The Wind Mage's support aura improves nearby friendly armor; Prima expressly says this can benefit units from other friendly clans, not only Stormbringers.[^storm][^support]

Their **Lightning Elemental** Titan has an explicit artificial origin: the **Ritual of Making** animated metal rods through magical-electrical experimentation. Their **Tesla Coil** defense attacks creatures not tuned to the clan's frequency. The sources establish those inventions, but do not credit Stormbringers with inventing the ancient Moongates.[^storm]

Faine's command of the weather also becomes a campaign obstacle. The guide describes machinery associated with rain, wind, and lightning around his fortress. These machines are part of the conflict, not merely atmospheric background art.[^weather]

### Plaguespitter — Syst's marsh alchemists

**Syst**, the fourth brother, rules a harsh society in the western marshes. Plants, poisons, acids, and chemical extracts connect the clan's original labor to its battlefield abilities.[^plague]

Their ordinary units are **Cropper**, **Savage**, **Bile Spitter**, **Shaman**, and **Spore Fiend**. Their specialties include corrosive damage, lingering acid effects, and weakening enemy armor. The Spore Fiend's armor-reducing support complements the clan's other attacks.[^plague][^acid]

Their **Green Slime** Titan dissolves material and grows as it feeds. It can expend accumulated size through an acid blast. The manual describes a kinship between Slimes and Plaguespitters; it does not say Syst created the species. Their **Hive** defense houses poisonous marsh flies.[^plague][^slime]

The guide makes another useful connection: it names designer **Dan Russett** while explaining the clan's acid upgrades. This is one place where the licensed book preserves a development explanation alongside gameplay advice.[^acid]

### Nighthorde — Naxus's scavengers and soul thieves

**Naxus**, the youngest commander, leads a clan that searches cursed battlefields and ancient burial sites. The manual depicts him participating personally, working among corpses beside his followers.[^night]

Their ordinary units are **Scrounger**, **Reaper**, **Cannibal**, **Grave Robber**, and **Witch Doctor**. Their identity combines scavenging, strong close combat, attacks on structures, and taking souls. The Witch Doctor projects a damaging aura with a chance to steal souls.[^night][^soul-thieves]

Their Titan is the **Battle Ball**, a spiked metal sphere that can collide with enemies, jump, and detonate. The manual also gives it an earlier practical use: preparing burial grounds for exhumation. Their **Soul Vortex** defense is described as an opening to a netherworld that extracts spirits.[^night]

Naxus's betrayal does **not** make every Nighthorde goblin a loyal accomplice. The campaign guide explicitly describes friendly Nighthorde defectors joining Grommel against him.[^defectors]

### The Wise One — one character, two titles, a false peace offer

The **Wise One**, also called the **Old Man**, is a Plaguespitter shaman. We have no established personal name for him. He claims some of his people are tired of Syst, takes **300 gold**, and offers Grommel a route toward Syst. It is a trap leading to **three Slime Titans**.[^wise]

His story demonstrates Grommel attempting negotiation. It does not independently prove that the peace faction the shaman describes actually exists. A non-hostile messenger introduces the encounter, but that messenger is also unnamed.[^wise]

## 4. The story, from beginning to end

The following account follows the **licensed Prima guide's campaign descriptions**, with the manual supplying the background. It is not a word-for-word transcript of the game's movies. Where an exact line or piece of cinematic choreography has not been checked, it is not presented as a quotation.

### Act I — the machine and the death of the creator

The opening places Fraziel and Naxus together at the Great Machine. Grommel's people are still working within that industrial world. The early missions introduce gathering resources and directing the Stonekrushers, then turn into a struggle to rescue workers and escape a mine catastrophe.[^act-one]

The guide describes Fraziel appearing and dying after the collapse. Later, Naxus admits the murder. That gives us a supported sequence without compressing every event into the oversimplified claim that the entire Great Machine exploded and vanished. The machine remains central to the end of the campaign.[^act-one][^act-five]

Some secondary accounts give Fraziel a final clue involving the word “brother.” The precise dying words still need a timecoded retail-scene check; they are not authenticated dialogue in this handbook.[^fraziel-research]

### Act II — Grax and the Hellfire

Grax blames Grommel for their master's death. Grommel's advance through the woodlands becomes a war against Hellfire infrastructure, defenses, and increasingly dangerous weapons. The conflict shows how a clan built around timber harvesting can turn its industry toward warfare.[^act-two]

At the end, Grax attempts a final explosive attack. Prima describes him being killed in his own gunpowder explosion while Grommel survives. The campaign moves forward with Hellfire forces available to Grommel. The broad outcome is supported; we still should not invent an unverified escape speech or exact movie transcript.[^act-two]

### Act III — Faine, the mountains, and the weather machines

The Stormlands introduce the Moongate ritual and then increasingly elaborate defenses. Faine opposes Grommel from his mountain territory. The obstacles include elemental attacks, teleportation infrastructure, and machines that manipulate the weather.[^act-three][^weather]

Prima describes Faine making a final lightning attack, Grommel surviving it, and Grommel killing him. Stormbringer becomes part of the force Grommel can command. Again, the guide's account is stronger evidence than an invented surrender or reconciliation scene.[^act-three]

### Act IV — poisoned fountains, a false truce, and Syst

In **The Marshes**, ordinary Soul Fountains are polluted and unusable. The guide connects that situation to a **Defiler Tree**. This matters both to the story and to gameplay: the clan's environmental corruption interferes with the resource that creates and replenishes armies.[^defiler]

The campaign continues through harvesting operations and into **Sacrifice**. There Grommel accepts an invitation from the Wise One, pays for supposed assistance, and is ambushed by three Slimes. The shaman's claim of opposition to Syst cannot be accepted at face value.[^wise]

Sacrifice also has an unusual reinforcement rule. Prima says that when all five goblins of one of the participating clans die, five replacements of that clan arrive, at a cost of **75 souls**. This is a mission-specific rule, not the standard army limit for the whole game.[^sacrifice]

In **Hive Mind**, Grommel confronts Syst. The guide describes Syst attempting to kill him, only for Syst's own carnivorous plant-pet to devour its master instead. The aftermath connects Naxus to Syst's campaign against Grommel. It does not establish that Naxus personally engineered every earlier accusation by every brother.[^syst]

### Act V — replicas, defectors, and Naxus's real plan

Naxus admits murdering Fraziel. Grommel then faces apparently familiar enemies from clans that have already come over to his side. The guide explains that Naxus is using **Fraziel's Moonstone** to make **soulless goblin replicas**, including copies of other clans' goblins, bound to serve him.[^act-five]

That explanation prevents a major misunderstanding: seeing a hostile Stonekrusher or Hellfire does not automatically mean the original clan has collectively betrayed Grommel again. It also does not identify every individual enemy as a replica. The source gives a mechanism, not a complete census of authentic versus copied goblins.[^act-five]

Grommel also encounters genuine Nighthorde opposition to Naxus. Friendly members of the clan offer to join him at a ruined Clanshrine. Their defection is explicitly described, unlike the Wise One's unconfirmed claims about dissent.[^defectors]

The return to the machine includes a very physical obstacle: a Slime must become large enough to operate a pressure plate. Pipes, machinery, and balloon-like components form part of the industrial environment. The machine is a traversable complex, not simply a mysterious object mentioned in dialogue.[^machine]

In **Purgatory**, Naxus reveals his plan to kill Ogriss's inhabitants by separating souls from bodies, then use those souls to create a new race in his image. Grommel must disarm **five bombs** at colored Moongates within **30 minutes**. The guide warns that the purple bomb can be rearmed by Nighthorde troops when dealt with too early.[^finale]

The listed playable clans for this final mission are **Stonekrusher, Hellfire, and Stormbringer**. Political unity across five clans is not the same thing as controlling five army groups at once.[^finale]

### The ending—and the limit of what we can claim

Prima says Grommel uses his wits to defeat Naxus. It then describes goblins living in peace and prosperity under Grommel, with the five clans united. Its introductory framing also calls him a prospective first goblin king.[^ending]

That supports **victory, unification, and a peaceful aftermath**. It does not independently establish a coronation scene or Naxus's permanent death. Secondary summaries describe a final exchange involving a Moongate and a bomb; the precise choreography and its implications still need direct retail-footage verification.[^ending][^fraziel-research]

## 5. How the world and the gameplay fit together

### Souls and gold do different jobs

**Souls** primarily pay for goblin units; **gold** is used with souls for such things as unlocking units and buying upgrades. You gather souls from defeated enemies, captured Soul Fountains, and rarer Soul Caches. Gold commonly comes from smashing objects and finding treasure.[^economy]

Prima gives a captured Soul Fountain's income as **2.5 souls per second** and a destroyed Soul Cache's reward as **150 souls**. Those are the guide's published values. They are useful test targets, not a claim that we have traced the exact implementation in every game revision.[^economy]

This is an important distinction for the fiction as well. Souls are not just an abstract score: they are collected, spent, stolen, cut off, and ultimately threatened on a planetary scale. But the game does not supply a complete explanation of where souls originate or what happens to every soul after it is used.

### The buildings

**Clanshrines** are the clan-specific places for purchasing ordinary goblins and upgrades. The **Hall of Titans** supplies Titans and defensive turrets. These major buildings generally belong to the level's starting layout; the game does not revolve around laying out a conventional strategy-game town from scratch. Damaged or destroyed buildings can be relevant to repair and rebuilding, which is different from freely placing a new base.[^buildings]

Neutral locations provide other advantages. **Soul Fountains** supply souls, **Alchemist Shops** sell Runestones, **Observatories** sell Moonstones, and **Scout Towers** reveal more of the map. Capturing a useful location can therefore matter as much as defeating an enemy squad.[^economy][^items]

There are also map-specific exceptions. On the GameCube **Graveyard** map, Prima identifies **Soul Wells** as major soul sources and says the **Observatories also act as Scout Towers**. That should not be generalized into a rule for every Observatory on every map.[^graveyard]

### Every clan has a counter

The five-clan relationship forms a complete circle:

**Stonekrusher → Nighthorde → Hellfire → Plaguespitter → Stormbringer → Stonekrusher.**

Here the arrow means “has the listed advantage over,” not “always wins.” Unit choice, upgrades, positioning, and support still matter. The circle is documented gameplay balance. The idea that Fraziel intentionally designed a perfectly balanced society is an interpretation, not an established piece of lore.[^counters]

The guide also distinguishes physical, clan-associated, fire, and structural damage and their corresponding defenses. That explains why two apparently similar attacks can perform differently against units and buildings. We should not turn a simplified description into an invented damage formula for the browser reconstruction.[^combat]

### Support units make combined armies meaningful

Drummers heal. Scouts extend vision. Wind Mages improve armor. Spore Fiends weaken enemy armor. Witch Doctors damage nearby enemies and can steal souls. These are different tools, not five differently colored copies of one support role.[^stone][^hell][^storm][^plague][^night]

The Wind Mage is a particularly clear connection between clans: its protection extends to friendly units regardless of clan. In the guide's explanation, its aura does not improve green armor, preserving the Plaguespitter advantage. Nighthorde's Witch Doctor and Soul Vortex offer different ways of interfering with the soul economy.[^support][^soul-thieves]

### Upgrades are not the same as unlocking a unit

A roster can contain a unit that has not yet been unlocked for purchase. An upgrade instead improves units or a support ability. Prima describes separate clan-specific upgrade categories, each with three purchased levels; the starting units are at level zero. Some clans improve armor or damage, while others improve health, speed, acid, or support effects.[^combat]

That is enough to understand the gameplay distinction. It does **not** settle how the internal technology values numbered 1–5 control every gate in the PS2 code. That remains one of our reconstruction questions.

### Moonstones: the creation artifact and the battlefield items

There are two uses of the same name in the sources. **Fraziel's Moonstone** is the artifact associated with creating goblinkind and with Naxus's replicas. **Ordinary purchasable Moonstones** are temporary battlefield boosts associated with particular clans.[^act-five][^moonstones]

Prima says the purchasable stones cost **15 gold** and triple the relevant clan's unit damage for **20 seconds**. They affect that clan's units across the map rather than requiring the player to target a small area. The book lists their colors as white, red, blue, green, and **black**.[^moonstones]

That last color is not a sixth clan. Nighthorde's moon **Farthis is purple** in the creation account, while its ordinary Moonstone and damage category are **black** in the guide. The final mission also uses a purple Moongate. The sources use different color conventions in those contexts; they do not explain a hidden additional moon.[^creation][^moonstones][^finale]

We still do not know whether the ordinary stones are fragments of Fraziel's artifact, related objects, or simply another use of the term.

### Runestones: portable spells

Runestones are consumable powers, found on maps or bought at Alchemist Shops. The guide describes two shared inventory slots for Runestones and Moonstones. Unlike a clan-wide Moonstone boost, a Runestone normally acts where the player targets it.[^items]

The documented set includes **Acid Cloud**, **Bonesaw**, **Earthquake**, **Firestorm**, **Freeze**, **Healing**, **Lightning**, **Shield**, **Slow**, **Soul Drain**, and **Transmogrify**. They cover attacks, protection, healing, slowing or immobilizing enemies, and soul theft. Earthquake has multiple described attack stages; that does not make it two differently named items.[^items]

**Transmogrify turns enemies into sheep.** The manual's playful mystery around the item should not be inflated into evidence of an undiscovered secret storyline.[^items]

### Moongates connect places, but their builders are unknown

The guide describes a **Master Moongate** linking same-colored **Slave Moongates**. In **The Ritual**, the blue moon **Trist** reaching its full phase drives the gate ritual, represented through the mission's timing. That is a concrete connection between a named moon and an important game system.[^moongates]

The guide also says **Titans cannot pass through Moongates**. This helps explain why moving a giant creature is not interchangeable with moving an ordinary squad. None of this tells us who originally built the network or proves that the Stormbringers invented it.[^moongates]

### Several soul systems—not one proven universal mechanism

Soul Fountains supply souls. Witch Doctors and Soul Vortices steal them. The Defiler Tree is tied to corrupted fountains. Fraziel's Moonstone makes living goblins; Naxus uses it for soulless copies. The final bombs separate souls from bodies.[^economy][^soul-thieves][^defiler][^act-five][^finale]

**Our interpretation:** the repeated concern with souls makes creation and control central themes of the setting. **What we cannot claim:** that all these devices were made by the same lost civilization, use an identical mechanism, or establish a complete theory of the afterlife.

A similar caution applies to weapons. The guide distinguishes a **one-off neutral Gunpowder Cannon** from the normal clan turrets. Not every dramatic machine encountered during the campaign is a standard purchasable Hellfire unit.[^cannon]

<a id="mysteries"></a>
## 6. The deeper lore: what remains unanswered

### Where did Fraziel learn his magic?

We do not know. Nighthorde searches for lost wisdom, but Fraziel already possessed creation magic when he created that clan. Its later search therefore cannot, by itself, explain where his original ability came from. Earlier expeditions, teachers, or other sources of knowledge remain possibilities—not discoveries.[^fraziel-research]

### Where were the other humans?

The manual refers to humans collectively, so saying it “never mentions humans other than Fraziel” is inaccurate. However, only Fraziel is personally identified as human in the story sources we have examined. We have no authenticated human homeland, capital, named ruler, teacher, or neighboring settlement.[^creation][^fraziel-research]

Their absence from the campaign is not evidence that all other humans were extinct, lived off-planet, or had been deliberately erased. Those are different hypotheses and need different evidence.

### Who left the ruins and ancient knowledge?

We have ancient battlefields, crypts, bones, and lost wisdom. We do not have the identity of the people who left them. A past **human empire**, an ogre civilization responsible for all ruins, or a named ancient kingdom would go beyond the recovered sources.[^night][^fraziel-research]

### Was Fraziel secretly planning the same genocide as Naxus?

This is one of the most important unresolved questions. On printed **page 99**, Prima describes Naxus perverting his master's machine. On **page 108**, it reports Naxus saying that making soul-severing bombs was always the machine's purpose.[^intent]

We should preserve both statements. Naxus's claim is not independent proof of Fraziel's motives, but the earlier description also does not prove Fraziel was innocent. A final script, recorded dialogue, or an attributable explanation from the writer could help distinguish deliberate ambiguity from a revision or guide inconsistency.

### What are the moons and the netherworld, really?

The five moons have names, colors, and creation associations. The Soul Vortex is linked to a netherworld. That is meaningful evidence, but it does not establish a pantheon, named gods, a sixth moon, an afterlife hierarchy, or the netherworld's inhabitants.[^creation][^night]

### What do replicas tell us about life and reproduction?

The sources distinguish created goblins from soulless copies. They do not explain a full goblin reproductive biology, establish that every goblin is male, or prove that Fraziel originally made souls out of nothing. Even a simple-seeming question—whether a replica yields souls when defeated—needs gameplay and story evidence rather than an assumption based on ordinary enemies.[^act-five][^fraziel-research]

### Did Naxus die, and was the ending a sequel setup?

His defeat is described. Permanent death, confirmed survival, and a documented sequel setup are not established by that wording. Developer intent may be recoverable, but an open ending and a later unsourced sequel database entry are not enough to prove an actual sequel was produced.[^ending][^sequel]

<a id="development"></a>
## 7. How the real game was made

### The people behind it

The production-credit research identifies **Paul Meegan and Ken Klopp** with the original concept, **Ron Millar** with lead design, **Chris Millar** with production, and **Daniel Russett** with co-lead design. **Christopher Zirpoli** is credited for writing and also contributed goblin voices. The credits identify **Kai Vilhelmsen** as narrator and **Lani Minella** with narrator casting and direction.[^credits]

The art research includes concept work credited to **Adam Adamowicz**, alongside the project's other art and production contacts. **Ray Lederer** has described texturing roughly a third of the game's characters and producing platform-specific versions; that is an artist's account, not a file-by-file asset count we have independently reproduced.[^credits][^art-history]

**Hal Hanlin** later described receiving a designer role on the game after doing well in an internal idea competition. His account explains how he joined the design team. It does not establish that his submitted concept was the original *Goblin Commander* pitch.[^hanlin]

### A console-first strategy game

Period developer material emphasizes controller-friendly group orders and the ability to control Titans directly. Chris Millar's **25 June 2003 diary** also discusses the difficulty of handling unexplored territory when the camera changes from an overhead view to a goblin's eye-level view. The team could not simply apply an ordinary overhead fog-of-war presentation without considering that change of perspective.[^publisher][^console-design]

This helps explain why the original design cannot be reproduced merely by placing familiar models on a map. Camera, group behavior, visibility, direct control, and the scale of the fighting all contribute to how the game feels.

### The art changed during development

In **July 2003**, Jaleco announced a **two-month visual enhancement involving a 24-person art team**. The official-site announcement promoted concept art, diary material, comparison images, and clan spotlights beginning with Stonekrusher.[^website]

That establishes a real art revision. It does not establish that the whole story was discarded or that every imagined early faction existed. The monthly spotlight schedule also does not prove that all five planned profiles were ultimately published and preserved.

### Early descriptions do not always match the finished story

The research found early press descriptions that present Fraziel differently, including language about controlling goblins or having his own clan. Early publisher material also described choosing clans before missions. These are genuine differences in **what was advertised or reported**; without a build or design document, they are not proof of an implemented alternate campaign.[^early]

The same caution applies to numbers. One early fact sheet advertised **25 single-player levels**, while later material gave **17 campaign missions and 29 total levels**. A Spanish article discussed **more than 40 unit types**, while launch material described **25 specialized goblin characters**. Different dates and counting methods may explain the differences. We cannot derive “eight cut missions” or a definite number of removed units by subtraction.[^early]

### Online multiplayer really existed during development

In a **2018 interview**, Chris Millar said online multiplayer worked on the team's PCs and on Xbox. His account attributes its removal to the constraints of releasing across platforms with different feature expectations and certification requirements.[^online]

This is stronger evidence than a rumor: it is a firsthand account of a working feature. It still does not establish a commercially released PC version, a hidden switch that restores online play in retail, or a surviving complete online build.

### Engine, animation, and audio technology

The credit research names **Truth Engine**, **Granny Animation**, **zlib 1.1.4**, and **Sonica** among the game's technology or middleware references. These help guide technical investigation, but credits alone do not identify which format, version, or code path applies to each extracted file.[^credits]

Likewise, platform-specific art variants mean a PS2 texture, Xbox texture, and GameCube texture should not be assumed interchangeable. A familiar-looking asset is not proof that its underlying layout or export settings are identical.[^art-history]

### Release, company history, and the sequel question

The original release belongs to **2003**. Jaleco's FY2004 filing records European releases on all three consoles during **2004**. The same filing reports a **¥975 million loss for the game business segment**; it does not attribute that entire loss to *Goblin Commander*. Our historical research also records the Millar brothers moving to Lionhead in June 2004.[^release][^corporate][^lionhead]

There are leads concerning discussion of a second game, including a reported Spanish magazine interview and an unsourced **Goblin Commander II** database entry. We have not authenticated a production announcement, funded development, or a sequel build. The project's MMO research also separates *Goblin Commander* from **Lost Continents**, another title in an artist's career history. Their appearance in the same biography does not make *Goblin Commander* an MMO.[^sequel]

<a id="archive"></a>
## 8. What our archive actually contains

### The strongest foundation

We have the original **Prima strategy-guide PDF**, its searchable page-indexed text, the original **Xbox manual**, and source-page images. We also have older research reports, source registers, the October 12 technical-session export and handoffs, later archive leads, and an outreach dossier. Their preservation and publication status is recorded separately; not every recovered raw file is public in GitHub.[^audit]

The **14 October audit** reconciled a register of **133 claims**, including two substantive findings that had been omitted when earlier claim IDs changed. It recorded **52 entries whose supporting passages were rechecked**, rather than declaring all 133 independently verified. It also reproduced **41 original passage captures with matching hashes** and indexed **85 proof-image records** in total. Two of the recovered passage captures share the same image bytes.[^validation]

In ordinary language: we can now point to substantially more of the actual source material, and we know more clearly which earlier statements were checked, corrected, or left open. A checksum proves that a file matches another file; it does not prove that every interpretation of its contents is right.

### The prototype and other game versions

Preservation records document an **Xbox prototype dated 26 September 2003** with a damaged source disc. The recorded provenance includes a DVD-R and more than one dumping method. The audit had not completed a file-by-file comparison against an identified retail revision.[^prototype]

A missing or broken file in a damaged prototype is not automatically cut content. We need to separate corruption from actual development differences. Magazine-disc listings also identify PS2 demos, and retail catalogs identify regional and language variants. Those are comparison opportunities—not proof that the localized story, objectives, or ending differ.[^versions]

### The most useful outstanding archive leads

The project has several concrete leads rather than just a generic instruction to “search old websites.”[^archive-leads]

| Lead | Why it matters | What remains missing |
|---|---|---|
| **Original English official website** | Clan profiles, concept art, diary entries, and comparisons could preserve material outside the manual. | A complete authenticated capture and asset inventory; especially any later Nighthorde profile. |
| **French official-site announcement, March 2004** | May lead to another set of site pages, downloads, and translated material. | Original destinations and surviving files, with translations compared against English. |
| **June 2003 screenshots** | Could show the game before or during its visual overhaul. | Original image bytes and verified filenames, not guessed numbered URLs. |
| **Trailers, TV commercial, and cinema advertisement** | May preserve early art, narration, or a different presentation of the story. | Original playable media and a frame/timecoded review. A résumé's cinema-ad reference still needs title-specific confirmation. |
| **TeamXbox, HomeLAN, and GameZone interviews** | Could recover direct explanations from the original developers. | The actual interview pages, not only articles linking to them. |
| **Nintendo Power and Official Xbox magazine coverage** | May clarify the redesign, strategy details, or sequel discussions. | Exact issue/page verification. The April 2004 tactics lead is not automatically the separate interview remembered in a 2003 forum discussion. |
| **Trademark and early announcement records** | Can help reconstruct the public naming and announcement timeline. | Rechecking of the exact official records; they do not by themselves establish present ownership or permission to reuse the game. |

The historical N-Europe and GamesDatabase listings are useful media leads, and the project has identified possible GameTrailers destinations. A listing or surviving hyperlink is not the same as having watched and preserved its video.[^media]

### What we should not quietly turn into canon

Fanfiction continuations are fanfiction. A database entry for a sequel or a possible handheld version is a lead, not a confirmed game. Generated portraits or maps are modern artwork, not recovered production art. A wiki, a mirror, and an AI-written report repeating the same summary do not create three independent sources.[^audit][^sequel][^archive-leads]

Some of our discoveries are good candidates for improving community wiki articles, especially the ogre war, the strange origins of the Titans, the Wise One's trap, and the Moonstone replicas. But we have not completed a current article-by-article wiki comparison, so we should not label all of them “missing from the wiki” without checking the relevant revision.[^wiki]

<a id="reconstruction"></a>
## 9. Our browser reconstruction, in plain English

### What we are trying to build

The recorded project goal is a **browser-native reconstruction of the version already being investigated, primarily derived from PS2 material**. It is not a request to switch to GameCube. An emulator can provide reference footage and behavior, but embedding an emulator or streaming the original game would not fulfill the native-browser goal.[^technical]

The GitHub repository containing this handbook is the **research archive**. It is not, by itself, the current browser-game source repository. The latest local implementation and the wave19 traces were not recovered in the audit, so this handbook does not claim the current game build has been tested or finished.[^audit][^technical]

### What earlier work reported

The **October 12 records** report an archive containing **17,235 entries**, **4,850 candidate Granny-related blobs**, and a small set of successful name checks. They also report a **65 × 65 terrain heightfield**, **29 archetype/model mappings**, and a scene placing roughly **200 units using five clan-body meshes**.[^technical]

Those are useful milestones, but the distinctions matter. Two hundred placed units are not two hundred separately decoded character models. A file that looks like an animation blob is not yet a verified, correctly playing animation. A terrain grid that loads is not yet a level whose heights, collision, lighting, and camera all match the original.

The same historical notes report **19/19 browser probes** and **26 parser tests** passing at particular points. They are dated test results, not evidence that every mission, win condition, sound, menu, and save behavior now works.[^technical]

### The seven main problems still tracked

| Problem | What it means without the technical shorthand | What would count as an answer |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Campaign objectives** | We can read some objective descriptions without yet knowing the exact condition the original code uses to complete them. | Match each objective to the original state change and show both a case that completes it and a similar case that does not. |
| **2. Technology, unlocks, and upgrades** | We must distinguish whether a unit is allowed, unlocked, affordable, available in the scenario, and upgraded. | Trace the actual rules instead of inventing multipliers or treating all the variables as one “tech level.” |
| **3. Shrines, rebuilding, and defeat** | Destroying a shrine is not necessarily the same thing as ending a match. | Document damage states, repair/rebuild possibilities, and the original conditions for a natural loss. |
| **4. Ownership on eight maps** | Some extracted objects do not yet have securely identified owners or teams. | Connect original entity identifiers to team assignments and reference captures—not just the nearest shrine or a guessed color. |
| **5. Visual measurement** | Something can load correctly but still be missing, duplicated, offscreen, or visually wrong. | Compare the same scene, camera, state, and visible object set, with an explanation of every mismatch. |
| **6. Animation and cinematics** | Models need correct skeletons, movement, cameras, timing, scale, and presentation. | Match original clips or scenes under controlled conditions, not merely produce plausible motion. |
| **7. Original reference states** | We still need reliable examples of important menus, saves, victories, defeats, credits, and audio behavior. | Capture the original state and reproduce it. An unobserved case remains unobserved, not passed. |

The dated objective report breaks **21 unresolved rows** into **0 exact, 6 inferred, 6 fallback, 7 data-absent, and 2 mechanic-absent**. That describes the recorded report, not a newly rerun count. The actual current table and traces are still needed.[^technical]

Earlier texture work explored palette ordering, PS2 memory transfers, and possible permutations. Those are historical hypotheses, not a solved texture decoder. The newer seven-problem handoff should not be displaced by automatically restarting an older experiment.[^technical]

### A valuable independent connection: gde's PS2 research

An independent researcher known as **gde / gdeOo** published PS2 disassembly-related work and a shared achievement script. Our technical audit pinned an exact revision rather than referring vaguely to whatever the repository contains later.[^ra]

The script distinguishes **mission objectives**, **mission script state**, **unit locks**, **upgrade levels**, **resource events**, and specific **shrine ownership groupings**. Those are promising points of comparison with our extracted data. In particular, separate fields for locks and upgrades reinforce the need not to treat them as the same concept.[^ra][^technical]

There are traps here too. The number **150** is used in a soul-cache resource-amount test; it is not the resource event's identifier. A **60-hit tally** in a Moongate achievement is the researcher's achievement timing, not proof that the original game has a 60-frame teleport rule. And addresses from one PS2 revision should not be applied to another until the build matches.[^ra][^technical]

The best next step is to compare **three things together**: a source description, the independent memory research, and our own trace from a named original build. Agreement between all three is much stronger than a plausible guess from any one alone.

### How we keep the work honest

Each experiment should preserve the input file and its hash, the command or method, the resulting trace or capture, and an explanation of what it proves. A failed experiment is worth keeping if it eliminates a hypothesis. An agent should do one bounded research cycle, update the evidence and next action, then let the external loop invoke it again.[^method]

For audio, “the sound file loaded” is not the same as “the sound was audible and matched the original.” For saves, destructive tests should use disposable copies of virtual memory cards, not somebody's only save. For progress reports, the denominator matters: “all assets complete” is meaningless without saying which assets were counted.[^technical]

## 11. Quick answers and corrections

| Question | Best-supported answer |
|---|---|
| **Was the original game from 2006?** | No. Its original release was in **2003**. |
| **How many clans?** | **Five**. The normal maximum under simultaneous player control is **three**. |
| **How many campaign missions?** | **17**, across five acts. |
| **Was the Wise One helping a real peace movement?** | He **claimed** dissent, then arranged a trap. The movement is not independently established. |
| **Were all Nighthorde loyal to Naxus?** | No. The guide describes **defectors joining Grommel**. |
| **Did earlier allied clans all betray Grommel again?** | Not established. **Soulless replicas** explain at least part of the opposition. |
| **Four or five final bombs?** | **Five in Prima**, with a 30-minute limit. Preserve the older fan-walkthrough discrepancy when comparing versions. |
| **Did Fraziel definitely plan genocide?** | **Unresolved**. The guide preserves conflicting descriptions and a claim made by Naxus. |
| **Do we know an ancient human civilization existed?** | The sources establish **ancient places and knowledge**, not the inhabitants' identity. |
| **Does the manual ignore other humans entirely?** | No. It refers to humans collectively, but does not locate a wider human society. |
| **Did Faine invent the Moongates?** | Not established. Operating or defending a technology is not proof of inventing it. |
| **Is Naxus certainly dead?** | His **defeat** is described; permanent death is not confirmed by the guide's ending. |
| **Was Grommel crowned onscreen?** | The guide's king/unification language does not authenticate a coronation scene. |
| **Was a sequel or MMO definitely produced?** | Not established by the recovered evidence. |
| **Is our generated map a canon source?** | No. It is a modern visualization unless independently matched to an authenticated original. |
| **Is the browser reconstruction finished?** | This research archive does not establish that. The current local code and traces were not audited. |

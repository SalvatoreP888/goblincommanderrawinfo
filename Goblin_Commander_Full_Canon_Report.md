## Full Canon & Research Report

# Executive summary

Goblin Commander is a story about created workers becoming a people of their own: five specialized clans, a murdered creator, a war between brothers, and a machine that threatens the souls of Ogriss.

This report consolidates the full story and the successive research findings from this conversation. It preserves the obscure details, but also corrects overconfident wording in the earlier summaries. The original release belongs to 2003, not 2006; the game appeared on PlayStation 2, Xbox and GameCube. [P1; W1]

## The strongest findings

The official material establishes artificial goblin origins, five moon-linked commanders, and a division of labor supporting Fraziel’s Great Machine. The manual adds a defeated Stone Ogre population, electrically animated constructs, biological weapons, and an opening into a netherworld. Contemporary publisher copy also explains the commanders’ spiritual projections. [M1, pp. 22–33; P2]

The central campaign follows Grommel from the destruction of the mines to the defeat of Naxus. Its broad narrative is well supported by published summaries. Some finer points—especially the final bomb count, the precise wording of Naxus’s creation plan, and a purported prosperous epilogue—were attributed earlier to indexed Prima passages that could not be independently re-read for this report. They are retained as qualified findings, not silently promoted to verified canon. [W1; R0; G1]

# How to read the evidence

A fact appearing on several websites is not automatically supported by several independent sources. A wiki, a mirror and a retrospective may all repeat one earlier summary. This report therefore separates the authority of an original source from the completeness with which it was inspected.

| Label | Meaning in this report |
| --- | --- |
| CONFIRMED | Directly visible in the inspected official manual or surviving publisher material. |
| SUPPORTED | Consistent campaign or gameplay information in secondary accounts; not a claim that every relevant scene was personally reviewed. |
| PROVISIONAL | A specific earlier finding whose underlying passage or footage remains incompletely verified. |
| INTERPRETATION | An analytical reading of established material, not additional fictional history. |
| OPEN | The inspected material does not settle the question. |

## Source priority

For final-game story details, the retail game’s actual dialogue and cinematics should take priority. The published manual is the clearest available reference for origins, faction identities and descriptive lore. An official guide is valuable, but its authority does not excuse relying on an incomplete excerpt. Pre-release marketing establishes what Jaleco said at a particular time; it is not proof that every advertised feature or premise survived unchanged.

Fan wikis, walkthroughs and forums are discovery tools and corroboration. They become especially useful when they preserve quotations from a vanished official page, identify a magazine issue, or point to an exact scene. Forum recollection alone cannot establish a cut storyline.

## What was checked for this compilation

The Xbox manual’s relevant spreads were visually inspected, including the creation account, clan entries, runestones and credits. Jaleco’s website-launch announcement, its story copy and developer diary preserved by LadyDragon, and the November 2003 Quarter to Three discussion were readable. Selected wiki pages and published summaries were also reviewed. [M1; P1; P2; F1; W1; W2]

The complete Prima PDF did not load for page-by-page inspection. Wayback capture pages were not successfully inspected. The video links remain playback leads rather than a verified transcript. Earlier claims based on those materials are explicitly qualified. This prevents a previous confident answer from becoming its own evidence. [G1; A1; V1; V2; R0]

> Bracketed source codes resolve in the source register. Manual page numbers are the printed numbers, not the PDF viewer index: the scan places two printed pages on most images.

# Ogriss and the creation of goblinkind

## An engineered society

Ogriss is the setting of the game; contemporary publisher copy explicitly calls it a planet. Fraziel is a human wizard who creates goblin laborers for the Great Machine. Their commanders supervise specialized workforces, and the early story description says they periodically report to Fraziel using spiritual projection. The premise begins with an organized system of production, not five clans already engaged in perpetual civil war. [P2]

The manual’s Chronicle of Being presents creation through the Moonstone and links the commanders to five colored moons. It places Grommel first and Naxus last in the sequence. These are magical origins; the text does not provide an evolutionary history, an astronomical model, or a full account of reproduction. [M1, pp. 22–23]

| Order / commander | Clan | Moon / color | Original work |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Grommel | Stonekrusher | Froxx / white | Stone and precious metals |
| 2. Grax | Hellfire | Heelinx / red | Forest harvesting |
| 3. Faine | Stormbringer | Trist / blue | Lightning and magical research |
| 4. Syst | Plaguespitter | Phoust / green | Plants, extracts and fuel |
| 5. Naxus | Nighthorde | Farthis / purple | Scavenging old battlefields |

Creation sequence and names: M1, pp. 22–23; corroborating overview: W1.

## Geography without an invented world map

The official account places Stonekrusher in northern volcanic mines, Hellfire in eastern forests, Stormbringer on southern mountain slopes, and Plaguespitter in western marshes. Nighthorde occupies cursed battlegrounds and crypts, but the inspected English manual does not assign that homeland a compass point. These are regional descriptions, not enough information to draw a reliable continental map. [M1, pp. 22–33]

## The narrator is part of the world

The Chronicle speaks collectively as a goblin, treats humans as a distinct group, and remembers service to Fraziel favorably. Its closing reference to the master’s language is a small cultural detail. The voice is unnamed; it should not automatically be identified as Grommel. [M1, pp. 22–23]

Interpretation: this framing matters morally. A servant’s nostalgia for clear purpose is not an omniscient declaration that the creator was benevolent. The later machine revelation can change how a reader understands that earlier loyalty without requiring the Chronicle itself to be false.

> Use the manual’s singular “Moonstone” when describing its wording. The relationship between that term and the five moons is not explained precisely enough to invent a system of five separate named artifacts.

# Stonekrusher & Hellfire

## Stonekrusher — Grommel

CONFIRMED. A mining clan associated with Froxx, the white moon. Its descriptive identity centers on durability, strength and industrial labor. Grommel is the first-created commander and the protagonist of the campaign. Stonekrusher’s army gives the player a physical, close-combat foundation rather than making every clan interchangeable. [M1, pp. 22, 24–25; W1]

Roster: Miner, Rock Thrower, Lugger, Pitboss and Drummer. Titan: Stone Ogre. Defensive structure: Rock Lobber. The Drummer’s support role is healing. These names are retained as a reference roster, not a reconstructed balance sheet. [M1, pp. 24–25]

The Stone Ogres were not simply manufactured as goblin weapons: the manual records their defeat in a war for the mines and subsequent subjugation. This is an actual pre-campaign conflict. It supplies no dates, named ogre kings, campaign locations or account of how the war began. [M1, p. 24]

Interpretation: Stonekrusher combines hard work and collective endurance with an uncomfortable history of domination. Grommel can become the story’s liberator without the clan’s past being innocent. His coalition should not be rewritten as a peaceful alliance formed entirely through diplomacy.

## Hellfire — Grax

CONFIRMED. Hellfire belongs to Heelinx, the red moon, and works the forests. Grax’s official portrayal emphasizes destructive mania; what his own people find amusing can look alarming to outsiders. The clan’s identity joins forestry, ranged weapons and explosives. [M1, pp. 22–23, 26–27; W2]

Roster: Lumberjack, Archer, Hunter, Bombardier and Scout. Titan: Warpigpult. Defensive structure: Lens Cannon. The Scout provides increased vision. [M1, pp. 26–27]

Two unusually specific details survive in the manual: the giant boars were gentle before Hellfire repurposed them, and the Lens Cannon uses tree-sap lenses to focus sunlight. The Warpigpult also serves the clan’s timber-clearing work. The forest is a source of technology, not merely a backdrop. [M1, p. 26]

SUPPORTED. Grax accuses Grommel of killing Fraziel. His defeat culminates in an explosive attempt to kill both brothers; Grommel survives and the Hellfire survivors join him. The fan biography describes spiritual transformation as Grommel’s escape. Exact choreography should be checked against the cinematic before quoting it as a scene transcript. [W2; W1]

> Avoid unsourced additions such as a named Stone Ogre kingdom, an explanation for Grax’s eye apparatus, or a declared technological level for all of Ogriss.

# Stormbringer & Plaguespitter

## Stormbringer — Faine

CONFIRMED. Faine commands the blue-moon clan associated with Trist. The mountain stronghold, magical research and lightning experiments define Stormbringer. Faine is depicted as highly capable and deeply arrogant; those are characterization details, not proof that he possesses unlimited knowledge of Ogriss. [M1, pp. 23, 28–29; W3]

Roster: Acolyte, Conjurer, Pyro Mage, Ice Mage and Wind Mage. Titan: Lightning Elemental. Defensive structure: Tesla Coil. The Wind Mage provides an armor benefit to allies. [M1, pp. 28–29]

The Ritual of Making is a named experiment associated with animating the metal rods of Lightning Elementals. Tesla Coils spare targets tuned to the clan’s frequency. These details establish a specific mixture of engineering and magic; they do not identify the physical mechanism or make every Stormbringer a creator equal to Fraziel. [M1, p. 28]

SUPPORTED. Faine opposes Grommel after the Hellfire conflict. His defeat removes another commander while bringing the surviving clan into Grommel’s coalition. Stormbringer use of Moongates is not evidence that it invented them. [W1; W3]

## Plaguespitter — Syst

CONFIRMED. Syst’s green-moon clan, associated with Phoust, lives in western marshes and specializes in toxicology. Its original contribution involves rare plants and volatile extracts for the Great Machine. Syst’s rule prizes strength and treats other clans with contempt. [M1, pp. 23, 30–31]

Roster: Cropper, Savage, Bile Spitter, Shaman and Spore Fiend. Titan: Green Slime. Defensive structure: Hive. The Spore Fiend weakens enemy armor; the Hive houses poisonous marsh flies. [M1, pp. 30–31]

Green Slimes dissolve organic and inorganic material, grow while feeding, and can release an acid blast. The manual describes kinship between slimes and their goblin counterparts; it does not explain whether the clan created the species. [M1, p. 30]

SUPPORTED / PROVISIONAL. The campaign ends Syst’s rule through a fatal encounter with his own carnivorous plant. Earlier research also attributed to Prima the revelation that Naxus directed Syst’s campaign against Grommel. That relationship is preserved here as provisional until the relevant guide page or scene is inspected in full. [W4; R0; G1]

> Plaguespitter is not a catch-all undead faction. Poison, acid, plant life and marsh ecology distinguish it from Nighthorde’s corpse scavenging and soul theft.

# Nighthorde & the five military cultures

## Nighthorde — Naxus

CONFIRMED. Naxus is the last-created commander, associated with Farthis, the purple moon. His clan lives among cursed battlegrounds and old crypts, recovering useful remains and forgotten knowledge. The manual portrays him as a hands-on leader who takes part in corpse scavenging beside his people. [M1, pp. 23, 32–33]

Roster: Scrounger, Reaper, Cannibal, Grave Robber and Witch Doctor. Titan: Battle Ball. Defensive structure: Soul Vortex. Witch Doctors can steal souls. [M1, pp. 32–33]

The Battle Ball is both an explosive weapon and a device used in preparing burial grounds for exhumation. The Soul Vortex is described as an opening to the netherworld that extracts spirits. These are stronger details than the generic claim that Nighthorde is “the evil clan.” [M1, p. 32]

SUPPORTED. Naxus is the campaign’s final antagonist and the perpetrator behind Fraziel’s death. Some Nighthorde goblins join Grommel against him. The story therefore distinguishes a corrupt ruler from a population capable of changing allegiance. [W1]

## What distinguishes the five clans

| Clan | Core craft | Titan category | Support identity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stonekrusher | Mining / heavy labor | Subjugated living giant | Healing |
| Hellfire | Forestry / weapons | Boar-mounted artillery | Vision |
| Stormbringer | Electrical magic | Animated metal construct | Armor |
| Plaguespitter | Toxicology | Corrosive organism | Armor weakening |
| Nighthorde | Scavenging / soul magic | Mechanical explosive | Soul theft |

Factual roster and equipment comparison: M1, pp. 24–33. Category labels summarize the descriptions rather than reproduce an official taxonomy.

Interpretation: the five armies resemble different working cultures militarized by the same crisis. Their weapons extend their occupations: extraction becomes demolition, forestry becomes artillery, research becomes electrical warfare, toxicology becomes corrosive combat, and scavenging becomes soul exploitation.

The manual’s counter-cycle is Stonekrusher over Nighthorde, Nighthorde over Hellfire, Hellfire over Plaguespitter, Plaguespitter over Stormbringer, and Stormbringer over Stonekrusher. This is a combat relationship—not proof of ancient wars, political rivalries or fixed social superiority. [M1, p. 20]

# The collapse and the first brother

The campaign begins as an investigation into a creator’s death and becomes a struggle over who has the right to direct goblin civilization.

## Act I — The Machine / Rumble-Squish

SUPPORTED. Fraziel’s Great Machine is sabotaged. The resulting explosion devastates the mines where Grommel and Stonekrusher have been working. Grommel encounters the dying wizard, whose last clue is the word “brother.” That clue directs suspicion toward one of the other commanders without immediately identifying the culprit. [W1]

The disaster removes both a ruler and the framework that gave the goblins their assigned purpose. Grommel’s initial problems are concrete: keep his people alive, escape the ruined underground complex, and discover what happened. The campaign’s early subterranean stages establish the home and labor system that the final act will revisit. [W1; R0]

PROVISIONAL. Earlier research attributed to Prima an opening cinematic showing Fraziel with Naxus before the Stonekrusher action begins. This is a potentially important detail of their relationship, but the relevant scene was not independently transcribed during compilation. It should remain a specific verification target rather than a newly certified revelation. [R0; G1]

## Act II — The Hellfire conflict

SUPPORTED. Grommel reaches Hellfire territory and encounters Grax. Rather than accepting his account, Grax blames him for Fraziel’s death. The ensuing fighting moves through the clan’s forest and industrial holdings. The surviving record describes disputes over resources, attacks on lumber operations and the destruction of gunpowder stockpiles. [W2]

Grax’s final act follows his established destructive character: cornered among combustible material, he attempts to destroy Grommel and himself. Grommel survives; Grax does not. The Hellfire survivors subsequently accept Grommel’s command. The precise use of spirit form in the escape is described by the fan biography and should be confirmed directly before reproducing the scene shot for shot. [W2; W1]

## What changes after Grax

Interpretation: Grommel has not simply collected a new unit roster. His investigation has now caused the death of a brother, while the people of that brother’s clan have become his responsibility. Success and moral cost advance together. That pattern is central to the campaign’s structure.

The story does not justify treating every Hellfire goblin as personally complicit in Grax’s decisions. Clan survivors can change allegiance, a possibility that later becomes decisive against Naxus. Likewise, Grax’s accusation should not be treated as evidence of guilt: it is an action by a character within the story, not an authoritative narration.

> Mission titles in this report are consolidated from the earlier walkthrough notes. The narrative is a synthesis, not an official script or a frame-by-frame cinematic transcript.

# The mountain war and the marshes

## Act III — Faine and Stormbringer

SUPPORTED. After the Hellfire conflict, Grommel’s coalition encounters Faine’s mountain-based Stormbringers. Faine is another brother who opposes him. The campaign’s escalation reflects the change in enemy: fortified mountain positions and magical infrastructure replace the forests and explosive industry of Hellfire. Faine is defeated and killed; surviving Stormbringers join Grommel. [W1; W3; W5]

The earlier walkthrough notes identify Moongate objectives, magical defenses, generators and a final stronghold assault across The Ritual, The Coming Storm, Death from Above and Thunderhead. These provide useful scene-level orientation, but this report does not convert them into exact trigger conditions or claim a particular generator is destroyed in a particular cutscene without a direct mission reference. [R0]

Faine’s official arrogance helps explain how the conflict can be read, but the earlier assertion that he repeatedly refuses surrender is stronger than the evidence inspected here. The secure account is opposition, defeat, death and the clan’s incorporation—not a fabricated sequence of negotiations. [M1, p. 28; W1]

## Act IV — Syst and Plaguespitter

SUPPORTED. The war then enters the marshes. Syst commands a clan whose weapons and environment are inseparable: toxic plants, caustic organisms and poisonous defenses. Published campaign summaries place his death before the final confrontation with Naxus. [M1, pp. 30–31; W1; W5]

The earlier walkthrough notes describe contaminated Soul Fountains, attacks on harvesting and refining operations, and the final Hive confrontation. The four recorded mission titles are The Blight, Harvest, Sacrifice and Hive Mind. These details are retained as research notes rather than a fully validated list of mission objectives. [R0]

Syst’s death is associated with losing control of a giant carnivorous plant. Earlier summaries simplified this to being devoured by his own plant; a secondary account describes a more direct physical fusion and violent separation. Until the footage is examined, the conservative formulation is that the plant turns fatally against him. [W4; R0]

## The Naxus connection

PROVISIONAL. The previous investigation reported an official-guide passage linking Naxus to Syst’s efforts to kill Grommel. This would be an important transition from a murder mystery to a directed pursuit of the surviving brother. However, neither the passage’s page nor its exact dialogue could be independently checked for the report, so the connection is not presented with the same confidence as the manual’s clan lore. [R0; G1]

Interpretation: the middle acts turn the five-clan labor system into a coalition through conflict. Each victory brings another set of skills under Grommel, preparing a final confrontation that depends on the combined civilization rather than on Stonekrusher strength alone.

# Naxus, the Great Machine and the ending

## Return to the place of creation

SUPPORTED. The final movement brings Grommel back toward the underground machine complex. Naxus is revealed as the brother who sabotaged the Great Machine and killed Fraziel, intending to take control of its power. Some Nighthorde goblins abandon Naxus and fight with Grommel, so the coalition ultimately represents all five clans. [W1]

The machine’s threat is not merely an ordinary explosion. Published summaries describe bombs intended to steal the souls of Ogriss’s creatures and enable its controller to remake the world. The Moongates provide the means to dispatch those weapons. This makes the final crisis an extension of the game’s existing soul economy rather than an unrelated last-minute device. [W1; F1]

## Fraziel’s plan is not identical to Naxus’s wording

The earlier answers sometimes collapsed their goals into one exact plan. The safer distinction is between the machine’s described capability and each character’s intended use. Fraziel created the labor force and commissioned the device; Naxus kills him and seeks to appropriate it. Precise statements about the population Fraziel hoped to create require dialogue or guide text beyond the general plot summary. [W1; R0]

PROVISIONAL. Earlier indexed-guide research described Naxus as intending to use the harvested souls to create a new race in his own image. That is more specific than simply ruling the existing goblins. It is retained as a reported guide finding, pending inspection of the actual page or matching cinematic. [R0; G1]

## Purgatory and the last confrontation

The research record agrees on a timed final mission but contains conflicting counts: an older walkthrough was reported as giving four bombs, while earlier Prima notes and a later walkthrough give five objectives. Five is the stronger working lead, not a source of invented certainty about all versions. A retail objective screen or complete guide page should settle the matter. [R0; G1; W6]

SUPPORTED. The commonly published ending has Naxus escape through a Moongate after leaving a live bomb near the others. Grommel throws the active bomb through the portal after him. The explosion implies Naxus’s death, but the account does not establish his confirmed survival or provide an unambiguous recovered body. Grommel then leads the united clans with the machine’s old purpose gone. [W1]

## What the ending does—and does not—prove

Unification under Grommel is supported. A specifically peaceful and prosperous future was previously attributed to Prima’s description of an additional closing cinematic; that stronger claim remains provisional. No later chronology is supplied here. [W1; R0; G1]

> Interpretation: the ending can be read as emancipation from a creator’s imposed purpose. It is not proof of democracy, the abolition of every hierarchy, or a permanently conflict-free future.

## What can be placed in historical order

Older history: the setting includes old battlefields, crypts and fallen warriors. These establish prior conflict and burial, but not a named lost empire. The word “ancient” is not a dated chronology, and the inspected text does not prove that every ruin predates the first goblin. [M1, pp. 23, 32]

Creation: Fraziel creates the five commanders and their clans, assigning complementary work for the Great Machine. Grommel is first in the creation sequence and Naxus last. No calendar year or interval between their creation is given. [M1, pp. 22–23]

Before the campaign: the clans establish their occupations and technologies. The Stone Ogres lose the mine conflict; Stormbringer conducts the Ritual of Making; Nighthorde scavenges old graves. Their relative dates are unknown. These are separate facts, not grounds for an invented centuries-long timeline. [M1, pp. 24, 28, 32]

Campaign: sabotage and Fraziel’s death precede Grommel’s wars with Grax, Faine and Syst, followed by the confrontation with Naxus. The coalition grows as the previous leaders fall. [W1]

Aftermath: the clans rally under Grommel, and the Great Machine no longer defines their shared work. The available story account does not furnish a successor list, later wars or a dated golden age. [W1]

## Naming discipline

Use Grommel, Grax, Faine, Syst, Naxus and Fraziel; use Stonekrusher, Hellfire, Stormbringer, Plaguespitter and Nighthorde. Spellings vary in casual retrospectives. This report follows the inspected English manual rather than treating obvious misspellings as additional characters or factions. [M1, pp. 22–33]

# Magic, death and the small details

## Souls are tangible resources, not a complete theology

Soul harvesting belongs to the original game design. A November 2003 forum preserves an official FAQ explanation connecting it to defeated goblins and captured Soul Fountains. The manual also names soul-draining magic and Nighthorde soul theft. These establish usable soul power; they do not explain whether harvested individuals remain conscious, remember previous lives or can be restored intact. [F1; M1, pp. 19, 32–33]

The Soul Vortex’s netherworld description is a genuine supernatural reference. It establishes an opening beyond ordinary physical space, but not a map of the afterlife, a pantheon, or proof that all dead beings reach the same destination. “Purgatory” is also a mission title; that alone does not turn it into a cosmological doctrine. [M1, p. 32; R0]

## Projection is not proven immortality

The publisher’s story copy explicitly describes commanders projecting spiritual forms to direct their workers and visit Fraziel. This gives the command perspective an in-world basis. It does not establish unlimited teleportation, possession of any creature, immunity to death or unrestricted travel between worlds. [P2]

## The small details worth preserving

| Detail | Safe significance |
| --- | --- |
| Tree-sap lenses | Hellfire forestry supplies optical weapon materials. |
| Stormbringer frequency | Selective Tesla targeting has a stated fictional explanation. |
| Ritual of Making | Stormbringer can animate metal constructs. |
| Battle Ball exhumation work | Nighthorde machinery has a corpse-recovery function. |
| Naxus scavenging with his clan | The antagonist also participates in ordinary clan activity. |
| Goblin Chronicle narrator | The creation account carries a cultural perspective. |

All six details are visible in M1, pp. 22–23, 26, 28 and 32.

## Runestones and the Transmogrify question

The manual lists a range of runic effects, including elemental attacks, healing, slowing, vision, freezing, shielding, soul drain and magical blades. Its Transmogrify entry deliberately leaves the effect mysterious. Earlier research attributed the sheep transformation to Prima; without the full passage or observed activation, this report retains that specific effect as provisional, not as a newly verified mechanic. [M1, pp. 18–19; R0; G1]

## Interpretation: the same power can build or consume

The strongest thematic connection is between creation and exploitation. Fraziel’s laborers possess distinct cultures and crafts, while the final threat treats living creatures as resources for someone else’s design. Naxus’s ambition mirrors that relationship. Grommel’s coalition offers a different direction, although the violence of its formation prevents a simple division into innocent workers and uniformly evil masters.

# What the archives actually establish

## The original website was a real lore resource

Jaleco’s announcement dated July 14, 2003, preserved by Nintendo World Report on July 16, identifies an official website containing concept art, comparison screenshots, developer diaries and monthly clan spotlights. Stonekrusher was the first announced spotlight, with the order intended to match the clans’ introduction in the game. This establishes what the publisher advertised—not that every planned profile was published or survives. [P1]

## A visual overhaul is documented

The announcement specifically describes two months of design enhancement by a 24-member art team. A November 18, 2003 forum post recalls a Nintendo Power interview about broader mid-development changes. The official announcement corroborates visual revision; the remembered interview remains a lead for the extent of gameplay or other changes. Neither proves an abandoned canon storyline. [P1; F1, post 15]

## The preserved developer diary explains design, not hidden mythology

LadyDragon preserves Chris Millar’s June 25, 2003 diary on fog of war. It describes the challenge of combining an overhead commander view with direct third-person control: unexplored terrain and unseen enemies had to be handled differently between the two modes. This is useful development evidence, but it should not be stretched into new claims about the supernatural limits of commander powers. [P2]

## What the forums contribute

Quarter to Three preserves a quotation attributed to the official FAQ explaining the streamlined economy and direct control. That contemporary quotation is more useful than an unsourced recollection because it records identifiable publisher language. The same thread also supplies the Nintendo Power interview lead. Other forums and fan discussions remain worthwhile discovery routes, but recollection, enthusiasm and repeated plot summaries do not create new canon. [F1]

## Credits point to the next research targets

The manual credits Chris “Zirp” Zirpoli as writer, Ron Millar as lead designer, Chris Millar as producer, and Paul Meegan with Ken Klopp for original concept. Cinematics credits include Chad Gleason and Weili Huang. These are historical credits, not verified present-day roles or confirmation that any person still holds development material. [M1, pp. 34–35]

> No complete Wayback copy, private design bible, discarded screenplay, unpublished sequel or creator interview has been recovered for this report. Research attempts and leads are recorded so they can be resumed without being mistaken for completed discoveries. [A1; R0]

## A practical recovery sequence

First, preserve the manual and a complete readable copy of Prima, noting edition and printed page numbers. Second, transcribe the campaign’s cinematics and important in-mission dialogue with timecodes, identifying platform, region and build. Third, investigate the official website’s captured HTML and linked assets, recording actual capture dates instead of assuming a dated archive URL was successfully retrieved.

Then inspect the relevant magazine pages and approach credited creators through verified public professional channels. Ask about surviving scripts, design documents, clan profiles and the scope of the redesign. A recollection can be valuable, but it should be recorded separately from a contemporaneous document. No outreach was performed as part of this report.

## Using this report for reconstruction or adaptation

Treat the clan identities, documented equipment and broad story as the reference baseline. Do not convert mission names or prose lore into executable objective conditions, numerical upgrades, damage formulas or shrine-destruction rules. Those require the actual game behavior or data. Unused assets, when authenticated, demonstrate development content; they do not automatically establish events in the released story.

## Overall conclusion

The most substantial discoveries are not a secret pantheon or a hidden sixth faction. They are concrete details that make the existing world richer: labor-derived technologies, a conquered ogre population, practical soul magic, a named creation experiment and a narrator whose loyalty shapes the account of goblin origins. The strongest overall reading is a civilization escaping an imposed purpose. The unanswered history of that civilization remains a research opportunity, not permission to invent canon.

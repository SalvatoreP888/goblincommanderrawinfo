*Goblin Commander: Unleash the Horde* was released in **2003**, for PlayStation 2, Xbox and GameCube—not originally in 2006. Its story follows Grommel, eldest of five goblin commanders, as he investigates the death of the wizard who created his people, defeats rival brothers, and confronts Naxus over a soul-harvesting Great Machine. The broad arc comes from the released game's story as retold in contemporary sources; the creator myth and clan backgrounds are recorded in the original Jaleco manual. # PART I — THE WORLD AND THE FIVE CLANS

## 1. Ogriss, Fraziel and the creation of goblinkind

Contemporary publisher material explicitly calls **Ogriss a planet**. The powerful human wizard **Fraziel** created five groups of goblin laborers to harvest materials for a mysterious **Great Machine**. Each clan worked in a separate region and was led by one magically created commander. The commanders projected spiritual forms to supervise their workers and, every few moons, to visit Fraziel and report progress. This explains the overhead commander perspective as part of the game's fiction rather than only a user-interface decision. [S03](https://www.ladydragon.com/a-2goblin.html)

The original manual's *Chronicle of Being* describes life imparted through the power of the **Moonstone** and a sequence of five moon-linked commanders. Its narrator uses a first-person collective goblin voice, recalls service to Fraziel favorably, refers to humans collectively and alludes to Fraziel's language. **The narrator is not named**, so the account should not simply be credited to Grommel. The sympathetic tone is the narrator's perspective, not proof that Fraziel was benevolent. [S01, pp. 22–23](https://www.gamesdatabase.org/Media/SYSTEM/Microsoft_Xbox//Manual/formated/Goblin_Commander_-_Unleash_The_Horde_-_2003_-_Jaleco.pdf)

| Order | Commander | Clan | Moon / color | Assigned labor |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | **Grommel** | Stonekrusher | Froxx / white | Mines, stone and metals |
| 2 | **Grax** | Hellfire | Heelinx / red | Lumber and forest harvesting |
| 3 | **Faine** | Stormbringer | Trist / blue | Lightning and magical research |
| 4 | **Syst** | Plaguespitter | Phoust / green | Herbs, chemicals and machine fuel |
| 5 | **Naxus** | Nighthorde | Farthis / purple | Ancient battlefields and forgotten knowledge |

Source: [S01, printed pp. 22–33](https://www.gamesdatabase.org/Media/SYSTEM/Microsoft_Xbox//Manual/formated/Goblin_Commander_-_Unleash_The_Horde_-_2003_-_Jaleco.pdf). In the manual's regional descriptions, Stonekrusher occupy northern volcanic mines, Hellfire eastern forests, Stormbringer southern mountain slopes, and Plaguespitter western marshes; Nighthorde occupy cursed battlegrounds and crypts without a confidently identified compass bearing.

## 2. Stonekrusher — Grommel

Stonekrusher are miners and durable frontline fighters. Their roster is **Miner, Rock Thrower, Lugger, Pitboss and Drummer**; their Titan is the **Stone Ogre** and their defensive artillery is the **Rock Lobber**. Drummers support the army through healing. The manual presents Grommel as the first-created brother, later the campaign protagonist. [S01, pp. 24–25](https://www.gamesdatabase.org/Media/SYSTEM/Microsoft_Xbox//Manual/formated/Goblin_Commander_-_Unleash_The_Horde_-_2003_-_Jaleco.pdf)

**Critical older history:** Stone Ogres were **not designed in a workshop**. They fought the Stonekrushers over the mines, lost, and were subjugated for use as living siege weapons. That establishes a documented conquest **before Fraziel's death**, limiting any assumption that the five clans lived in an entirely peaceful society. The account provides no ogre kings, political institutions, war dates or named battles. [S01, p. 24](https://www.gamesdatabase.org/Media/SYSTEM/Microsoft_Xbox//Manual/formated/Goblin_Commander_-_Unleash_The_Horde_-_2003_-_Jaleco.pdf)

## 3. Hellfire — Grax

Hellfire are forest-workers turned aggressively experimental artillery specialists. The roster includes **Lumberjack, Archer, Hunter, Bombardier and Scout**. Their Titan is the **Warpigpult**, their defensive structure the **Lens Cannon**, and Scouts supply battlefield vision. The clan's leader, Grax, is portrayed as volatile and destructive. [S01, pp. 26–27](https://www.gamesdatabase.org/Media/SYSTEM/Microsoft_Xbox//Manual/formated/Goblin_Commander_-_Unleash_The_Horde_-_2003_-_Jaleco.pdf)

Two manual details are easy to overlook: the enormous boars used for Warpigpults were **formerly gentle creatures** repurposed for siege warfare, and the Lens Cannon's concentrating lenses are made from **tree sap**, turning woodland materials into energy weapons. Do not convert the latter into a modern semiconductor or laser specification not stated by the manual. [S01, p. 26](https://www.gamesdatabase.org/Media/SYSTEM/Microsoft_Xbox//Manual/formated/Goblin_Commander_-_Unleash_The_Horde_-_2003_-_Jaleco.pdf)

## 4. Stormbringer — Faine

Stormbringer are mountain-dwelling researchers who combine arcane practice and electrical engineering. Their army includes **Acolyte, Conjurer, Pyro Mage, Ice Mage and Wind Mage**. They wield **Lightning Elementals** as Titans and **Tesla Coils** as defenses. Wind Mages support allied protection. Faine is characterized as intelligent and arrogant. [S01, pp. 28–29](https://www.gamesdatabase.org/Media/SYSTEM/Microsoft_Xbox//Manual/formated/Goblin_Commander_-_Unleash_The_Horde_-_2003_-_Jaleco.pdf)

The **Ritual of Making** is a named magical-electrical process that animated arrangements of metal rods into Lightning Elementals. Tesla Coils selectively spare targets tuned to Stormbringer frequency. This establishes both artificial constructs and a clan-specific targeting concept; it **does not** establish that Stormbringers created ancient Moongates. [S01, p. 28](https://www.gamesdatabase.org/Media/SYSTEM/Microsoft_Xbox//Manual/formated/Goblin_Commander_-_Unleash_The_Horde_-_2003_-_Jaleco.pdf)

## 5. Plaguespitter — Syst

Plaguespitter are a marsh society specializing in chemical extracts, corrosive substances and dangerous plants. Their roster includes **Cropper, Savage, Bile Spitter, Shaman and Spore Fiend**. Their Titan is the **Green Slime**; their **Hive** dispatches poisonous marsh flies. Spore Fiends weaken an enemy's armor. Syst is depicted as harsh and contemptuous of weakness. [S01, pp. 30–31](https://www.gamesdatabase.org/Media/SYSTEM/Microsoft_Xbox//Manual/formated/Goblin_Commander_-_Unleash_The_Horde_-_2003_-_Jaleco.pdf)

The Green Slime devours or dissolves material, grows as it feeds, and can release an acidic blast. The manual describes a **strange kinship** between Slimes and Plaguespitters. It does **not** confirm that Syst created the Slimes. This contrasts with the explicitly manufactured Lightning Elemental. [S01, p. 30](https://www.gamesdatabase.org/Media/SYSTEM/Microsoft_Xbox//Manual/formated/Goblin_Commander_-_Unleash_The_Horde_-_2003_-_Jaleco.pdf)

## 6. Nighthorde — Naxus

Nighthorde occupy cursed, ruined battlefields and burial sites. Their units include **Scrounger, Reaper, Cannibal, Grave Robber and Witch Doctor**. Their Titan is the explosive mechanical **Battle Ball**; their defensive weapon is the **Soul Vortex**. Witch Doctors steal souls from enemies. [S01, pp. 32–33](https://www.gamesdatabase.org/Media/SYSTEM/Microsoft_Xbox//Manual/formated/Goblin_Commander_-_Unleash_The_Horde_-_2003_-_Jaleco.pdf)

The manual explicitly portrays Naxus joining his people in the search through **corpses**. The Battle Ball was used not only for warfare but also to prepare **burial grounds for exhumation**. Most strikingly, the Soul Vortex is described as an **opening to the netherworld** that extracts spirits. This supports a supernatural netherworld concept, but tells us almost nothing about its geography, inhabitants or metaphysical rules. [S01, p. 32](https://www.gamesdatabase.org/Media/SYSTEM/Microsoft_Xbox//Manual/formated/Goblin_Commander_-_Unleash_The_Horde_-_2003_-_Jaleco.pdf)

## 7. Society, magic and technologies

**Souls and gold.** Souls are collected through combat and certain world structures, then spent on goblin armies and certain creatures. Gold comes from map resources or destroyed structures. **Soul Fountains, Moongates, Clanshrines, Titan Halls, Observatories and Alchemist Shops** form the game's military landscape. Their gameplay roles are documented in the manual and period guides; their construction dates or ancient designers are generally **not** established. [S01](https://www.gamesdatabase.org/Media/SYSTEM/Microsoft_Xbox//Manual/formated/Goblin_Commander_-_Unleash_The_Horde_-_2003_-_Jaleco.pdf) · [S06](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/ps2/589779-goblin-commander-unleash-the-horde/faqs/26986)

**Moonstones are not automatically the same object.** The manual also calls temporary clan-attack power-ups purchased through Observatories or found on maps **Moonstones**, recognizable by symbols of the five moons. Prima later refers to **Fraziel's Moonstone**, the stone with which he originally made goblins. It has not been established whether they are identical objects, variations, or a broader class. [S01, p. 17](https://www.gamesdatabase.org/Media/SYSTEM/Microsoft_Xbox//Manual/formated/Goblin_Commander_-_Unleash_The_Horde_-_2003_-_Jaleco.pdf) · [S02, p. 101](https://www.ogxbox.co.uk/media/com_eshop/attachments/Goblin_Commander_Unleash_The_Horde_Strategy_Guide_2004_Book.pdf)

**Runestone mystery explained.** The manual treats the **Transmogrify Runestone** playfully as mysterious; the Prima guide's described effect is to turn enemy goblins into **sheep**. This is not evidence of a secret hidden lore quest. [S01, pp. 18–19](https://www.gamesdatabase.org/Media/SYSTEM/Microsoft_Xbox//Manual/formated/Goblin_Commander_-_Unleash_The_Horde_-_2003_-_Jaleco.pdf) · [S02](https://www.ogxbox.co.uk/media/com_eshop/attachments/Goblin_Commander_Unleash_The_Horde_Strategy_Guide_2004_Book.pdf)

**Five-way combat countering.** The manual's clan strength cycle is Stonekrusher over Nighthorde; Nighthorde over Hellfire; Hellfire over Plaguespitter; Plaguespitter over Stormbringer; Stormbringer over Stonekrusher. This is a game-balance relationship, not proof of old wars among every pair of clans. [S01, p. 20](https://www.gamesdatabase.org/Media/SYSTEM/Microsoft_Xbox//Manual/formated/Goblin_Commander_-_Unleash_The_Horde_-_2003_-_Jaleco.pdf)

# PART II — THE CAMPAIGN: FULL STORY & THE 17-MISSION INDEX

## 8. Plot overview

Fraziel's machine is sabotaged; his death leaves goblin society leaderless. As Stonekrusher commander Grommel tries to uncover who killed him, rival clan commanders blame or oppose Grommel and force successive campaigns through forests, mountains and marshlands. One brother, Naxus, proves to have betrayed Fraziel and appropriated the Great Machine. Grommel ends the story leading goblins from all five clans against a plan to kill living beings and harvest their souls. [S02, campaign sections](https://www.ogxbox.co.uk/media/com_eshop/attachments/Goblin_Commander_Unleash_The_Horde_Strategy_Guide_2004_Book.pdf) · [S39](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goblin_Commander:_Unleash_the_Horde)

### Act I — Stonekrusher: The Machine; Rumble-Squish

An explosion tears through the mine complex where Grommel's people serve the Great Machine. The survivors flee as Fraziel lies dying. The wizard gives the cryptic clue **"brother"**, prompting suspicion that one of the remaining commanders murdered him. Grommel undertakes to save his clan and solve the mystery. Some period guide notes mention an opening cinematic with Naxus and Fraziel, but an exact scene transcript has not been produced in this research. [S39](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goblin_Commander:_Unleash_the_Horde) · [S02](https://www.ogxbox.co.uk/media/com_eshop/attachments/Goblin_Commander_Unleash_The_Horde_Strategy_Guide_2004_Book.pdf)

### Act II — Hellfire: Woodland Workzone; Oink, Oink, Boom!; City in Ruins; Grax's Plaything

Grax blames Grommel for Fraziel's death, and the quarrel becomes open warfare. Grommel advances across Hellfire timber holdings, industrial facilities and fortress defenses. Grax ultimately attempts a final explosive attack that kills him but does not kill Grommel. The surviving Hellfires enter Grommel's coalition. The exact cinematic mechanism of Grommel's escape should still be checked directly against footage. [S02, Act II](https://www.ogxbox.co.uk/media/com_eshop/attachments/Goblin_Commander_Unleash_The_Horde_Strategy_Guide_2004_Book.pdf) · [S09](https://goblincommander.fandom.com/wiki/Grax)

### Act III — Stormbringer: The Ritual; The Coming Storm; Death from Above; Thunderhead

The conflict next reaches Faine's mountain region. Stormbringer defenses employ electrical and elemental magic, Moongates and fortified installations. Faine opposes Grommel and is defeated; his remaining people become part of Grommel's forces. Do not manufacture a speech or surrender offer where the surviving transcript has not been checked. [S02, Act III](https://www.ogxbox.co.uk/media/com_eshop/attachments/Goblin_Commander_Unleash_The_Horde_Strategy_Guide_2004_Book.pdf) · [S09](https://goblincommander.fandom.com/wiki/Stormbringer)

### Act IV — Plaguespitter: The Blight; Harvest; Sacrifice; Hive Mind

Grommel enters toxic western marshes, contests clan harvesting operations and pushes toward Syst's Hive. In the published plot account, Syst's own dangerous plant becomes instrumental in his death. The exact choreography is not established in the evidence preserved here. [S02, Act IV](https://www.ogxbox.co.uk/media/com_eshop/attachments/Goblin_Commander_Unleash_The_Horde_Strategy_Guide_2004_Book.pdf) · [S09](https://goblincommander.fandom.com/)

**Newly located political episode — the Wise One.** In **Sacrifice**, the Prima guide lists an objective to find a Wise One and bring **300 gold** to his hovel. Its commentary identifies the Wise One as a **Plaguespitter shaman** who says some tribal members are exhausted by Syst's reckless war and want peace with fellow goblins. A peaceful messenger also seeks contact with Grommel. The shaman directs him toward Syst. Thus the supplemental guide documents **internal Plaguespitter dissent and attempted negotiation**, not merely a military invasion. These statements are indexed guide text; the game dialogue should be extracted to upgrade them to A-level canon. [S02, pp. 90–92](https://www.ogxbox.co.uk/media/com_eshop/attachments/Goblin_Commander_Unleash_The_Horde_Strategy_Guide_2004_Book.pdf)

The guide additionally identifies **Naxus's involvement in Syst's campaign against Grommel**, an important connection between the marsh story and the final antagonist. The specific cutscene/dialogue remains a verification target. [S02, Act IV aftermath](https://www.ogxbox.co.uk/media/com_eshop/attachments/Goblin_Commander_Unleash_The_Horde_Strategy_Guide_2004_Book.pdf)

### Act V — Nighthorde: Hell Boils Over; Night Makes Right; Purgatory

Naxus's armies include familiar goblins that bewilder Grommel because their clans have already rallied to him. The Prima guide gives an explicit explanation: Naxus has obtained **Fraziel's Moonstone** and is creating **soulless replicas** of Stonekrusher and Hellfire goblins, sworn to follow him. This is **not** proof that the original clan members all rebelled anew. [S02, p. 101](https://www.ogxbox.co.uk/media/com_eshop/attachments/Goblin_Commander_Unleash_The_Horde_Strategy_Guide_2004_Book.pdf)

Nighthorde dissenters eventually assist Grommel. His forces return to the Great Machine, where Naxus's plan is to deploy bombs that sever souls from the bodies of living creatures. The guide explicitly says he means to use the souls to **build a new race in his own image**. This is more precise than saying he merely wants to rule Ogriss. It also must not be casually substituted for Fraziel's own less-fully-specified original objective. [S02, pp. 101, 108](https://www.ogxbox.co.uk/media/com_eshop/attachments/Goblin_Commander_Unleash_The_Horde_Strategy_Guide_2004_Book.pdf)

**Final mission facts:** Prima identifies **five colored Moongates / bomb objectives** and a **30-minute limit** for disarming **five bombs**. It lists **Stonekrusher, Hellfire and Stormbringer** as the playable clans for Purgatory, even though the political coalition contains all five. It is inaccurate to conclude every hostile goblin belongs to a newly rebelling clan. [S02, pp. 107–108](https://www.ogxbox.co.uk/media/com_eshop/attachments/Goblin_Commander_Unleash_The_Horde_Strategy_Guide_2004_Book.pdf)

**Ending:** Contemporary plot summaries describe Naxus escaping through a Moongate after leaving a bomb; Grommel redirects the danger through the portal. Naxus is no longer seen and his exact fate is **not explicitly confirmed**. The guide frames Grommel's goal as a peaceful, strong united nation and even says victory would make him the **first goblin king**. This is the guide's political framing—not independent proof that a detailed coronation scene appears. [S39](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goblin_Commander:_Unleash_the_Horde) · [S02, pp. 4, 108–109](https://www.ogxbox.co.uk/media/com_eshop/attachments/Goblin_Commander_Unleash_The_Horde_Strategy_Guide_2004_Book.pdf)

## 10. Questions the evidence doesn't settle

## 17A. Early Fraziel narrative versus the shipped game

GameSpot's **March 19, 2003 reveal** frames Fraziel as a wizard taking goblins under his control and disrupting Ogriss. A separate E3 preshow report, written as coverage of the 2003 show but displaying a later publication timestamp, goes further and describes an **evil Fraziel commanding his own goblin clan** as the player's enemy. These are genuine **pre-release press descriptions**, not a discovered script, prototype scene, or evidence that an alternate final campaign was completed. The press description conflicts with the murder mystery of the released version; resolving whether that arose from early plans or inaccurate reporting requires earlier draft scripts and capture comparisons. [S13](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/jaleco-reveals-goblin-commander-unleash-the-horde/1100-2912504/) · [S14](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/goblin-commander-e3-2003-preshow-report/1100-6026705/)

## 17B. Verified lead-designer interviews and development process

A Spanish *MeriStation* article attributes descriptions of the controller-first design to **co-lead designer Daniel Russett**. The report explains that the strategy layout, group controls, possessed Titan controls, and progressive campaign clan introductions were designed around console hardware, rather than being a simple mouse-driven PC game transplanted to a gamepad. It also claims **more than 40 goblin varieties**, compared with Jaleco's later **25 specialized goblin characters**; these are different historical descriptions, not proof of 15 removed units. [NEW02 — MeriStation](https://as.com/meristation/2003/12/25/avances/1072335600_010225.html) · [Jaleco Dec. 2003 release](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/pr/9213/the-horde-is-unleashed)

The historical URL is `https://www.teamxbox.com/article.php?id=690&t=i`. The existence of the interview, not its unpublished contents, is the verified fact. [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/29217/late-night-consoling).

## 17C. Character-art variants, engine technology and audio credits

Artist **Ray Lederer's** published professional history says he textured approximately **one-third of the characters**, worked with the exporter, and prepared **multiple versions of character assets for different platforms**. His own résumé independently places him at Jaleco and identifies *Goblin Commander* as a shipped credit. The artist's work history establishes platform-specific art production, but not specific removed characters, textures, or missions. [Work history](https://www.mobygames.com/person/70173/ray-lederer/) · [Artist résumé](https://raylederer.artstation.com/resume).

The credited technologies are **Truth Engine**, **Granny Animation**, **zlib 1.1.4**, and **Sonica**. Those names are directly useful for technical preservation searches, but do not identify which files or functions depend on any particular library. MobyGames' recorded GameCube credits also name original-concept contributors **Paul Meegan and Ken Klopp**, lead designer **Ronald Millar Sr.**, co-lead **Daniel Russett**, writer **Christopher Zirpoli**, concept artist **Adam Adamowicz**, narrator **Kai Vilhelmsen**, narration director **Lani Minella**, and development team members who performed goblin voices. [Original credits transcription](https://www.mobygames.com/game/11019/goblin-commander-unleash-the-horde/credits/gamecube/).

## 17D. Sequel discussion versus evidence of actual sequel production

There are multiple independent *mentions* of a sequel possibility, but none that by themselves authenticate a funded project. A Spanish *Official Xbox Magazine* issue has been cited in earlier research as carrying Ron Millar's aspirations for a potential second installment with online play. Its page image has **not been re-inspected in the present consolidation** and should remain a high-priority verification target. The later OGDB listing titled **Goblin Commander II (2004)** is a third-party database record with no cited publisher announcement. Separate Jaleco-associated work on the MMO **Lost Continents** does not make it a Goblin Commander sequel. [Magazine record](https://archive.org/details/Xbox_RO_21) · [OGDB listing](https://ogdb.eu/index.php?section=title&titleid=43631) · [Mike Hines interview](https://www.mmorpg.com/interviews/dev-profile-mike-hines-art-director-2000114613).

## 17E. What the 2003 marketing numbers do — and do not — imply

Early Xbox-era fact sheets advertised **25 single-player campaign levels**, whereas Jaleco's December 2003 GameCube release explicitly advertised **17 missions and 29 total single/multiplayer levels**. A modern archive also specifies **12 multiplayer maps**, providing a plausible explanation for 17+12=29. Treat the early count as a **documentary discrepancy** until original map catalogs or prerelease builds are compared. No eight cut campaign missions have been proven. [Early fact sheet](https://www.xboxhome.co.uk/xbox-games/xbox-goblin-commander-unleash-the-horde) · [Jaleco 2003 release](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/pr/9213/the-horde-is-unleashed) · [PS2 archive](https://www.gamesdatabase.org/game/sony-playstation-2/goblin-commander-unleash-the-horde).

## 17F. Concrete technical-preservation lead: RetroAchievements disassembly

A 2026 RetroAchievements developer discussed disassembling the PS2 game and later linked an unfinished `.rascript` project, saying it contained useful information about how the game worked. This is **not official lore**, but it is a specific public research artifact to check for symbol names, executable state, objective triggers, and game logic. [RetroAchievements forum, February–August 2026](https://retroachievements.org/forums/topic/32991?comment=415058).

## 17G. Improved source-hygiene and limitations

A source **URL is not the same thing as a preserved original**. The original Xbox manual was read visually in earlier investigations but could not be downloaded directly during this update. Prima's full 128-page PDF exceeds the browser retrieval limit; some specific passages, including printed page 101 on replicas, were available from its indexed text. No original manual or guide page images were archived into the `EVIDENCE/` folder in this deliverable. The independently accessible Jaleco press releases and Game Developer interview were directly read, and the February 2005 Jaleco corporate filing was directly read including the original printed page 9. This dossier therefore distinguishes **source located**, **text inspected**, **image inspected**, and **original artifact archived**, rather than treating them as equivalent.

The accompanying `Goblin_Commander_Claims_Evidence_v3.csv` contains a separately numbered entry for **each documented finding or bounded research conclusion**, with source IDs, directly clickable URLs, exact page/section or recording locator when known, verification scope, and a caution wherever the material is preliminary or uncorroborated.

Every claim is tagged with one of the following classifications: **A = shipped game**, **B = Jaleco/manual official lore**, **G = Prima licensed guide**, **D = documented development/release history**, **L = archival lead or unresolved claim**, **H = hypothesis or negative-research conclusion**. A phrase in a prerelease interview remains development history even when the speaker worked on the game.

## Original-source and screenshot policy

For wiki citations and future research, the goal is a pair of files per source-backed claim: **(1) the permitted, unaltered original source or faithful archived capture; (2) an unedited screenshot of the exact passage**, including enough page context to establish the source identity, plus URL, capture date, hashes, and exact page. The `EVIDENCE/` package supplied here contains the evidence inventory and capture queue. It **does not pretend that inaccessible copyrighted PDFs, videos, or source screenshots have been downloaded or preserved**. The live URLs and exact locators form the auditable source trail for now; an agent with access can fill the missing original + screenshot pairs.

## Verification limits that must be retained downstream

- **Primary manual:** relevant pages were seen in earlier research; direct PDF retrieval was unsuccessful on 14 October 2026. Page numbers refer to printed pages.
- **Prima guide:** full-file inspection blocked by PDF size. Specific passages are search-indexed; strongest indexed passage on p.101 explicitly discusses Naxus, Fraziel's Moonstone and soulless replicas. Other guide claims need actual page captures.
- **Original cutscenes:** links are included, but no complete timed dialogue transcript was generated in this run. Do not present timestamp precision that has not been independently read from footage.
- **Fandom wiki:** previous comparisons relied partly on indexed results; treat "not on the wiki" as **candidate gap** until inspecting the current page and its revisions.
- **Sequel and MMO:** hope, database records and separate projects do not prove sequel development.
- **Lost websites:** a Wayback index or historical URL proves a search route; it does not prove a particular capture has been recovered.

## What this report does and doesn't constitute

This is a **sourced synthesis** and research-navigation artifact, not an official publisher bible. It contains no new fictional events invented to close the gaps. It preserves the evidence available, including contradictions. Citations are kept on individual paragraphs, source IDs map to a browsable source register, and the CSV makes it possible to investigate every numbered claim independently.

# FINAL ASSESSMENT

The original game offers a stronger documented mythology than most short summaries suggest: magically manufactured workers, a politically fractured five-clan society, ancient graves and a defeated ogre population, labor-derived military technologies, a named artificial-life ritual, and weaponized souls. The original guide further adds internal Plaguespitter dissent, a Wise One advocating peace, Naxus's counterfeit soulless armies, and a five-bomb finale. Beyond the fiction, surviving development records establish a significant art overhaul, removed functional online multiplayer and an unusually valuable—but partly corrupted—Xbox prototype. [S01](https://www.gamesdatabase.org/Media/SYSTEM/Microsoft_Xbox//Manual/formated/Goblin_Commander_-_Unleash_The_Horde_-_2003_-_Jaleco.pdf) · [S02](https://www.ogxbox.co.uk/media/com_eshop/attachments/Goblin_Commander_Unleash_The_Horde_Strategy_Guide_2004_Book.pdf) · [S04](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/pr/8827/goblin-commander-website-open) · [S19](https://hiddenpalace.org/Goblin_Commander:_Unleash_the_Horde_(Sep_26,_2003_prototype)) · [S21](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/reversing-the-sunk-cost-fallacy-devs-recount-what-shouldn-t-have-been-cut)

**What remains absent is equally important:** no verified sixth clan or god pantheon; no established ancient-empire chronology; no confirmed Naxus survival; no authenticated cut story mission from the prototype; and no reliable proof a commissioned sequel entered production. These are open research targets, **not missing chapters to invent**.
